## Who is this guy and why should I care?

<img src="/contents/public/profile.jpg" align="left" alt="MEEEE!">

Hi, I'm Eric Mackrodt and I can't tell you why you should care, that's up to you,but here are a few things about me.

I was born in Brazil in 1988 and lived there until 2015, when I moved to Australia, where I currently live and recently became a citizen. I'm also a German citizen because of my grandpa. I've been to Germany twice and I love it, but sadly I don't speak any German and I've never lived there.

I've been into technology since I was a kid. I used to disassemble anything I could get my hands on like TVs, radios, computers, you name it.

Growing up we didn't have much money, so a lot of the time I would get electronics as hand downs from my uncles and aunties. If they weren't working well, I would just take them apart. Because of that, there were loads of things I wanted to have and play with but couldn't such as laptops, Gameboys, handheld PCs, and so on.

Now that I have some disposable income, I'm going back and buying those things so I can finally experience them for the first time. I'm also playing with stuff that brings me nostalgia, like retro video-games, retro computers, and other things that I or my cousins/friends owned and I played with a lot.

I started [The Eric Experiment](https://www.youtube.com/user/EricMackrodt) channel on YouTube so I can share those things with the world.

## Computers, Retro computing, retro mobile computing and beyond

Like I said, I was always into technology, computer especially because with them you can do anything, work, play games, create art or whatever.

And when it comes to PCs, desktop computers specifically, the DIY aspect has always been part of it, and considering I've always loved disassembling and assembling electronics, being able to build my own computers was always a huge plus.

Most of my computer usage in the 90's were through my uncle J and uncle O, they were the ones that had access to those cool things.

The first one, which I have very faint memories of was like a normal-ass tower, probably a 386 or a 486, with one of those anti-static, anti-radiation monitor filters or whatever that's called which belonged to uncle J.

That was the computer on which I played my favorite game of all time for the first time, Lemmings. 

Around this time my uncle J also had a laptop, one of those beige ones with a monochrome LCD screen where DOS would show with a white background and black text, as opposed to the usual white text on black background. This one he kept away from the kids for obvious reasons. We'll come back to it later.

The PC that I loved the most though was a 486 IBM Aptiva with all IBM peripherals and Windows 3.11 For Workgroups installed originally that my uncle J got a little later in the 90's.

On that computer I remember playing the Lion King Activity Center. Uncle J also had Corel Draw 4 with the clip-art CD and I loved going through them, selecting the ones I liked most, then using text on Corel Draw or Wordart on MS word to add some text to it and printing them to take home. That computer was also the one on which I played Full Throttle for the first time, which is also one of my favorite games of all time.

Yes, there is a correlation between nostalgia and those games being my favorites of all time. Those are the games that bring me comfort as they are the ones from my childhood.

Anyway, while playing the early game I used to keep right-clicking so then Ben would say "I don't have anything", my 8~10-year-old self thought that was hilarious.

That was, if I'm not mistaken, the computer on which I experienced the World Wide Web for the first time. Where I created my first email account (zipmail.com.br), used a lot of IRC and searched for dinosaur, gaming and computer things.

Around this time, I want to say circa 1997~1998, my uncle O had a computer store, NetBox, and we got a second-hand PC from him. I don't remember the specs, but it ran Windows 95 and had a black and white display. God I miss black and white displays... no they are not better, they are just lovely.

I don't remember much of what I did on that computer but I do know that we didn't have much in terms of software except for the stock Windows 95 stuff so I'd explore the OS to see what I could find, play solitaire and draw on Paint, I used to draw these picture frames, I'd use the spray can to make a colorful background and draw a smiley face on top of it.

Anyway, that computer broke down eventually, so we sent it back to be fixed, but for some reason that I never understood, we never got it back.

At that time Uncle O was living for a little while in our little backyard house, what do I call those? In Australia we'd call it a Granny flat maybe, but I don't feel it's the same, maybe an annex? It was a tiny self-contained 2 storey thing, it wasn't connected to the main house. I loved going there when no one was living there and pretending it was my house. Anyway, this is a tangent.

So uncle O lived "with us" for a little while and he had a more powerful computer, which I'd use sometimes. I also remember he had this laptop with the trackball next to the screen on the lid, I don't remember much about that one, I just remember really wanting to play with it.

Anyway, around that time I got another desktop. I think it was older than the previous one, it was probably a 286 or a 386, running Windows 3.1.

I was like... 9 to 10 years old. I remember adding icons in the Program Manager thinking that's how you started making computer programs. I also remember making little mazes in Paintbrush and drawing a little spaceship that I would drag around pretending that I made a game. Another thing that I'd constantly do is run the Windows 3.1 tutorial because I didn't have any games or much other software.

My memories from that time are a bit fuzzy. It's been nearly 30 years... oh my god.

But one thing that is etched in my memory is the sound of a dot matrix printer that I had with that computer. It was so much fun to be able to print things out, and at that age I would print a lot of silly things that weren't important at all, but it was a lot of fun. I miss having a dot matrix printer. I love the noise it makes, but that's the nostalgia speaking because I'm sure that noise can be very obnoxious for people who are not used to it.

Eventually I ended up disassembling that computer just to see what was inside, just to look at the parts and stuff, and never managed to put it back together in a working condition again.

Not long after that, my dad was working with my uncle. They worked together and co-owned a computer school, so they obviously had a lot of computers. I remember one night being at home when my dad called me saying, "Eric, I have a surprise for you." I went downstairs and he had a new desktop computer, one of those flat horizontal case computers. Of course I was mind blown that my dad brought that home for me.

I think it was a bit newer than the computer I just talked about. I don't remember exactly what it was, but I'm sure it was still obsolete for the time because it was around 1998 and by then, if I'm not mistaken, Pentiums were already out, so it would have been like another 386 or 486 or something like that. Of course I promptly took it upstairs to my room, connected it to my monitor, set it up, and started using it. Funny enough though, I have fewer memories of this computer than I had of the previous one, but one thing's for sure: I ended up disassembling that computer as well and doing God knows what with it.

So, going back to that laptop I mentioned earlier that my uncle had, the one around the time of the Lemmings computer. Eventually the hard drive stopped working, and I don't know if he ever bothered to get it fixed, but around the time that my dad brought a computer home from the school, my uncle gave me that laptop.

And I cannot describe to you how awesome that was because, first of all, it was a laptop, and just the fact that I had one was amazing, even though it didn't have a hard drive with an operating system. I was happy enough to just turn it on and play around with the BIOS even though nothing really happened. I know I was around 10 and I would have known that the computer was broken and that it would have been kind of boring. But you've got to understand that I didn't have much money and I didn't have the means to get proper computers, so I made do with what I had, and that was enough for me to have some fun.

Later on, a school friend showed me that I could boot into DOS with a Windows 98 boot disk, and that opened up a whole new world of possibilities. That laptop became a lot more fun at that point because I could actually play some DOS games. I played a lot of Indy 500, I played Lemmings of course, Battle Chess, I started dipping my toes into "programming" by making batch files. It was amazing.

That computer was handed down to my family at around 2000 or 2001. At that point it had Windows 95 on it.

I'd still use it to go on the internet, I vividly remember searching for a Pokémon Black which I heard the rumor that it was a Pokémon game that wasn't released in Brazil.

At that time I was starting to make HTML pages and whatnot and I started playing around with QBasic and Delphi

It ended up breaking down eventually and it was promptly disassembled later on.

I have since got the same computer, which I need to inspect and see if it's working, and separately I got the keyboard, which needs repairs, and mouse. I'm just missing the speakers and monitor, which I hope to one day get so I can get that computer working in the same way 

I have a modern desktop PC, an UnRaid NAS and a couple of other computers that I built or modified myself, and that's always fun, but in terms of tinkering, what I really love are retro computers.

Anything with ISA/PCI slots, IDE/Floppy drives, VGA ports... Yummy Yummy in my tummy.

It's a mix of the nostalgia, plus now I can have computer parts that I wanted when I was young but I couldn't have.

God I miss black

Also there's the fun challenge of getting those computers to do useful things in the modern world. Especially when it comes to internet use.

Mobile retro computing, on the other hand, is peak "stuff that I wanted but didn't have money to get them". I used to look at them, especially handheld PCs and be mind blown that something so small and portable could do a lot of the stuff desktop computers could.

To me, they tasted like the future... and they still do, trust me, I bit one the other day and it still had the same taste, even if a lot of those form factors changed a lot over the years and some of them are completely abandoned by the industry.

There are so many iconic devices from that era, like the Cassiopeias, Jornadas, Psions, Palms and so on. But there also old smartphone and tablets, like the Nokia N95, N800, Blackberries, HTC phones with Windows Mobile and so on.

Sure, modern smartphones can do infinitely more than those devices could ever do, but there was so much variety and innovation at that time compared to all of the black rectangles that we get today, all of that was so new and exciting, and they weren't constantly trying to destroy our mental health and empty our wallets after we purchased them.

Those were all incredible devices in their own right, the vast majority I just briefly interacted with because someone I knew had one or I saw something about it in a magazine.

## Coding

I'm a software engineer by trade and I've been working in the field for 15 years (as-of Mar 2026). And a most of my personal projects involve some level of coding, if not the entire project.

To me, programming is one of the coolest things you can do. You can literally create amazing things out of nowhere, just by typing on your keyboard some instructions for the computer to follow. You can make the computer display graphics, play games, play music and so on, just by typing on your keyboard. Your imagination is the limit.

The only thing you're doing is flipping bits in your memory and hard-drive and in the end you have a fully interactive... thing!

That's why I see programming as more of an art than a science.

I kinda hope I don't work with it professionally for the rest of my life, though, because there are certain aspects of the corporate world and the software industry that turn me off, even though I'm working at a really good place right now. But I can say for sure that I'll probably keep programming to some capacity for the rest of my life because it's a really cool skill to have if you're a tinkerer and it can be very enjoyable.

## Brain waves

In terms of my personality, I'm a fairly agreeable person and I joke a lot. I tend to get along well with everyone, and I can be extroverted depending on the situation.

That's not to say I'm great at social situations. I do struggle a lot with dealing with large groups of people, especially if I don't know them very well or at all.

I'm the kind of person who gets exhausted if I'm in social situations for too long and eventually needs to go home and hide. I also have trouble reading the room in social situations.

It doesn't help that I struggle with depression and am (actually) diagnosed with ADHD as well, which makes it harder to deal with those things and to find motivation to work on my projects, my day job, and so on. Legend says (by legend I mean psychologist) that I have traits that indicate that I might be dipping my toe in *the spectrum* but I haven't been able to a do professional assessment on that so for now, I am not a in the spectrum. 

The funny thing about depression is that people think it manifests itself as sadness. That can be the case, but it's not true for everyone.

I'm not necessarily a sad person. I'm loud, I'm silly, and I can have fun. For me it manifests as more of a sense of constant dread and heaviness in my head. When it's really bad, it makes me feel physically heavy, not feeling the energy to move or anything like that.

But it's not sadness, it's like a pain that is not being physically manifested. Regardless of how it feels it certainly causes me not to enjoy things and not to have the energy to do things that would be fun otherwise.

It can also cause you to have a lot of difficulty with self-control, which in my case manifests as me being fat. I'm technically obese, and being obese doesn't help with the mental health issues.

Now, I don't want to be a bummer, but I think it's important to talk about those things openly because a lot of people suffer from mental health issues and aren't aware that there are people around them who have similar struggles. It's easy to feel alone when dealing with those things.

## Who am I, really?

But everything I said above are things that are common in our society to describe a person. I think saying definitively what makes you... you is a lot harder to do than that.

How can you actually define who someone is? What defines me? What makes me, you know, me?

That's a question that's really hard to answer.

Is it your sexuality, gender, ethnicity, or nationality?

I don't think so, because there are many people who have those exact same characteristics.

Is it our physical body?

I don't think so. This is a bit like the [Ship of Theseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_Theseus) because humans replace all of their cells every 7 years or so. So I have no cells in my body that were there when I was 18.

Is it the things I like? No, because there are probably hundreds or thousands of people who enjoy the exact same things I do.

Is Eric my personality, the way I behave and the way I feel?

We're probably getting closer here, but there are again hundreds or thousands of people with similar personalities and behaviors.

Some people would say that you are your memories, and I think that's closer to it. But at the same time, I'm 33 now and when I was 18, I hadn't had many of the experiences I've had so far, so I have a lot more memories than I had then.

But that means that 33-year-old me isn't the same person as 18-year-old me.

And what if I got amnesia for the rest of my life and didn't remember ANYTHING from my life before that?

Is that still me or is that a different person?

Or what if I have serious dementia, Alzheimer's, or something else when I get old? Is that still me or am I gone at that point?

And what if I got cloned right now? Would the second Eric be me as well? Definitely not after we had our first experience from different perspectives after the cloning, but could you argue that as soon as the second Eric appeared, we are both the same person for a few milliseconds until our brains can register the stimuli coming from the outside world?

Like, if I got teleported and the process for teleporting was me being destroyed on one side and being perfectly reconstructed on the other side—is that person on the other side still me or is that someone else, and did I just die when I entered the teleporting machine?

Or, what if my mind was uploaded to a computer as I was dying? Would that uploaded me still be me?

What about the parts of my personality that are tied to my DNA and stuff? Even if those things were emulated on the computer, would that still be me?

Is it even possible to define who a person really is?

I don't know. I can't answer any of those questions and it's probably a mix of all of the above.

And this ended up becoming more of me pouring my thoughts out.

Well, I guess those crazy thoughts also kind of define who I am as well... so...

Anyway, if you read this whole thing... I'm so sorry, but at the same time, thank you!
